Nigeria Pulse Check, October 2026

Africa's largest population is living through its most consequential economic experiment in a generation. The governance verdict turns on a question nobody in Abuja wants to ask first: who is paying for the transition?

The diagnosis

Nigeria's governing story of the past three years is, at bottom, a test of a thesis. Remove distortionary subsidies, let an artificially supported currency find its level, and the economy will eventually restructure toward productivity. The administration staked its mandate on that thesis in its first weeks. The fuel subsidy removal of May 2023 and the naira's flotation that followed were the two most consequential economic decisions in Nigeria in twenty years, taken with a decisiveness even critics acknowledged. [1]

The governance question is not whether the thesis is right. It is who has paid for the test.

Removing the fuel subsidy pushed up fuel prices and household transport costs. The National Bureau of Statistics recorded food inflation of 37.77% year-on-year in September 2024. That is a historical reading, not an October 2026 inflation estimate. Currency adjustment added pressure on imported inputs. Cash transfers and other support measures belong on the same ledger as the reform; their coverage, timing and adequacy determine who bears the transition. [2]

The other side of the ledger deserves attention. The Nigeria Customs Service reported substantially higher collections in 2024, while National Bureau of Statistics reports allow state-level internally generated revenue to be examined. Higher nominal revenue is not, on its own, proof of stronger real service capacity. A security verdict requires a dated, geographically comparable incident series, not a single national headline. [3] [4]

The institutional problem

Nigeria's deepest deficit is not economic policy. It is the distance between law and enforcement, which shows up at three levels.

Audit. The diagnostic question is what happens after an Auditor-General report is tabled. Unsupported expenditure, where established by an audit, requires a documented institutional response. A tabled report is a starting point. The public should be able to follow the response from the finding to a lawful outcome.

Procurement. Tender splitting, expansive single-source justifications and beneficial-ownership opacity are questions for contract-level scrutiny. These are investigative priorities, not findings about particular contracts. Establishing a pattern requires the contract sample, a reproducible method and responses from the institutions involved.

Elections. Results transmission became a credibility issue in the 2023 election. The institutional question remains: can the public inspect the design, failure records and remedies? A technical failure does not itself establish that officials intended or benefited from it. That conclusion requires evidence of intent, not an inference from failure alone.

The prescription

Three measures would do more for governance credibility in Nigeria than any macroeconomic reform now under discussion.

First, follow a documented audit finding through a lawful process to a public outcome. Select the case on evidence, allow a right of reply and distinguish a missing voucher from proof of criminal wrongdoing. A completed accountability process says more than another unexamined audit.

Second, require beneficial-ownership disclosure at the moment of contract award, not at company registration. Transparency only constrains when it arrives in time to matter.

Third, legislate redundancy into results transmission: parallel, real-time, public release of polling-unit results through independent channels, so that no single failure point can turn a glitch into a legitimacy wound.

Fact box

  • May 2023: fuel subsidy removal, followed by exchange-rate reform
  • Historical inflation reference: September 2024 food inflation of 37.77%, not the current October 2026 rate
  • Administrative record: compare official customs and state-revenue reports with inflation and service outcomes
  • Watch item: electoral preparedness and transparent, resilient results transmission
